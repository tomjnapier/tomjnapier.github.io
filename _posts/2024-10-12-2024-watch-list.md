I'm not massively into body horror, and I'm still a little disturbed from my uninitiated viewing of _Midsommar_ a few years back. At least this time a had a little warning about _The Substance_'s more graphic elements. 

![Image]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/the-substance.webp)

It's a story about beauty and age, and societies' sickness of disavowing anyone with not enough of the former and too much of the latter. 

Demi Moore gives a full on performance and the movie doesn't seem to pull a single punch. It's as engaging as it is gross.

### Alien: Romulus

Hmmm. Credit where it's due, Alien: Romulus doesn't repeat a lot of the same missteps as high-falutin' and boorish Prometheus/Covenant prequels, but it does this by trying to recapture what made the 1979's seminal _Alien_ so enduring. 

_Romulus_ has it's moments. The drudgery of the low-classed workers, trying escape their circumstances plays out with plenty of room to create a bit of suspense. And the visual style, whilst doing nothing to mimic the claustrophobic corridors and the shaky, nervous camerawork of the original, at least tries to ground itself in some real environments.

Alien: Romulus really disappointed when that grounding was completely lost with an unnecessary and clumsily executed character return. The film unspools itself from there, even crudely shoe-horning references to the aforementioned sequels, but it was a good ride for a while.

### The Bear

"Behind, BEHIND!"

I was a little late to the pass with The Bear, but got to tuck into the first two seasons before having the season three for dessert. It's a super amped-up drama, with literal and metophorical pots boiling over in pretty much every episode.

Season three opens with a beautifully realised montage of chef Carmy's career to date. It's moody, reflective, and deeply reverent of the kitchen. That kind of sums up the vibe of The Bear. It's an examination of some flawed and frustrating characters who somehow come together over this thing they love.
